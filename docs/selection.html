Model selection

1 Model complexity and information criteria

The covariance definition of degrees of freedom and prediction-error corrections provide the statistical background (Efron 2004). Stein’s identity (Stein 1981) relates divergence to Gaussian degrees of freedom under suitable regularity and integrability conditions. For the Lasso, see Zou, Hastie, and Tibshirani (2007); for partly smooth regularizers, including the fixed-weight Group Lasso, see Vaiter et al. (2017).

AIC (Akaike 1974) and BIC (Schwarz 1978) motivate the information criteria below. Substituting an effective divergence into these criteria does not transfer all their classical guarantees to an arbitrary adaptive penalized fit.

The coefficient count nzero is distinct from divergence. The latter includes penalty curvature and derivatives of adaptive weights. In grouped and adaptive output, df_fixed is evaluated at the same adaptive solution; it is not the complexity of another separately fitted model.

For \(d_\lambda=\widehat{\mathrm{df}}_\lambda\) and residual sum of squares \(R_\lambda\), the profiled criteria implemented when sigma2=NULL are

[ =n(R/n)+2(d_+1), =n(R/n)+(n)(d_+1). ]

The extra parameter is the estimated variance; common likelihood constants are omitted. These substitute effective complexity for parameter count: they do not assert ordinary maximum-likelihood AIC or BIC theory for every penalized candidate. See the R documentation for AIC for the underlying likelihood convention.

With a supplied variance \(v\), the likelihood term becomes \(R_\lambda/v\) and the variance parameter is not counted. SURE for mean estimation is

[ R_/n+2v d_/n-v. ]

sure <- select(fit,criterion='SURE',sigma2=.5^2)
sure$lambda
#> [1] 0.03
plugin <- select(fit,criterion='SURE',sigma2='ols')
plugin$variance_source
#> [1] "full-design OLS plug-in"

"ols" uses the full design and requires positive residual degrees of freedom. It is a plug-in criterion, not an exact known-variance SURE identity. Known-variance unbiasedness requires Gaussian errors and the relevant global regularity/integrability hypotheses; a numerical local divergence does not establish those hypotheses for adaptive overlap and sparse variants. Nor does unbiasedness at fixed lambda extend automatically to a selected fit. Failed diagnostics and exceptional-set conventions are excluded from selection.

2 Cross-validation without pilot leakage

Cross-validation and prediction-error estimation are discussed by Hastie, Tibshirani, and Wainwright (2015) and Efron (2004). Model search can contribute additional degrees of freedom (Tibshirani 2015), so a divergence computed at a fixed tuning value is not automatically the degrees of freedom of the data-selected procedure.

foldid <- rep(1:3,length.out=n)
cv <- cv.lmreg(X,y,foldid=foldid,penalty='adaglasso',groups=groups,
              profile='exponential',intercept=TRUE,standardize=TRUE,
              lambda=c(.3,.15,.07,.03),control=control)
print(cv)
#> SPARES cross-validation; alignment: lambda 
#>     rule lambda       mse
#>  minimum   0.07 0.2818870
#>   one-SE   0.30 0.3110961
plot(cv)
coef(cv,s='lambda.1se')
#>                     s=0.3
#> (Intercept)  1.062105e+00
#> x1           1.526346e+00
#> x2          -1.012827e+00
#> x3           5.910858e-01
#> x4          -5.559435e-03
#> x5          -4.291376e-10
#> x6          -5.713648e-10

Each training fold computes its own scales, OLS pilot and adaptive weights. Reuse foldid to compare methods. Supplied weight vectors must be fixed; passing weights calculated from the full response would introduce leakage. CV assesses prediction error and does not use DoF. Rank requirements for adaptive pilots must hold in each training fold, not just in the full data.

lambda.min minimizes pooled out-of-fold squared error. lambda.1se is the largest candidate within one displayed standard error of that minimum. The standard error is a between-fold dispersion summary, not an exact sampling uncertainty accounting for overlapping training sets.

If no absolute grid is supplied, each fold constructs its own automatic geometric grid; candidates are aligned by the same fraction of each fold’s maximum lambda. No full-data response is used to set training-fold grids. The reported lambdas then refer to the full-data refit. This distinction between absolute lambda and path-relative alignment is also discussed in glmnet’s cross-validation documentation.
